♡ HEART RATE

How to measure

Use an approved heart-rate monitor while resting.

Why it matters

Monitors cardiovascular activity and physical stress.

Possible risks

Unusual readings may require further assessment, especially when accompanied by dizziness or chest pain.

◉ BLOOD OXYGEN

How to measure

Place an approved pulse oximeter on your finger and wait for a stable reading.

Why it matters

Estimates oxygen saturation in the blood.

Possible risks

Unexpected readings or breathing difficulties require prompt medical assessment.

♨ BODY TEMPERATURE

How to measure

Use an approved digital thermometer according to its instructions.

Why it matters

Helps identify possible illness and temperature regulation problems.

Possible risks

Unusual temperature readings may indicate a condition that needs medical attention.

BLOOD PRESSURE

How to measure

Use an approved blood pressure monitor and follow its positioning instructions.

Why it matters

Helps assess the circulatory system.

Possible risks

Unexpected readings may require repeat measurements and medical review.

RESPIRATORY RATE

How to measure

While resting, count complete breaths for 60 seconds.

Why it matters

Helps monitor breathing patterns.

Possible risks

Serious breathing difficulties require immediate emergency assistance.

☾ SLEEP ASSESSMENT

How to measure

Record sleep duration and quality in Daily Check-in.

Why it matters

Sleep supports memory, recovery and concentration.

Possible risks

Persistent sleep deprivation can affect alertness and mission performance.

⚠ EMERGENCY RESPONSE GUIDANCE

If an astronaut experiences severe symptoms:

  1. Activate the mission emergency response protocol.
  2. Notify the crew medical officer and Mission Control.
  3. Assess the astronaut's condition.
  4. Provide assistance according to medical training.
  5. Follow mission-specific emergency procedures.

MISSION CONTROL / CREW WELLNESS

CREW MENTAL HEALTH

Supporting psychological wellbeing and cognitive performance throughout space missions.

PSYCHOLOGICAL WELLBEING SYSTEM

A healthy mind supports a successful mission.

Long-duration space missions can expose crew members to isolation, stress, disrupted sleep and demanding working conditions. ASTROGUARD promotes prevention, early awareness and continued support.

01 / PREVENTION DURING THE MISSION

🌙

Sleep Management

Maintain a consistent sleep schedule and allow sufficient rest to reduce fatigue and support concentration.

🏃

Physical Activity

Follow the mission exercise programme to support physical wellbeing and help manage stress.

🤝

Social Connection

Maintain supportive relationships with crewmates and communicate regularly with family when possible.

🧘

Stress Management

Practise relaxation, breathing exercises and enjoyable activities to manage mission-related stress.

🎯

Structured Routine

Balance work, rest, recreation and personal time to maintain a stable daily routine.

💬

Open Communication

Encourage crew members to discuss concerns and seek support without fear of judgement.

02 / EARLY WARNING SIGNS

Changes that may require attention

  • Persistent sadness, anxiety or irritability
  • Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
  • Unusual changes in sleep patterns
  • Withdrawal from crewmates or social activities
  • Unusual changes in behaviour or motivation
  • Difficulty completing normal mission tasks

These signs do not automatically indicate a psychiatric disorder. Persistent, severe or concerning changes should be reported to the mission medical or psychological support team.

03 / CONTINUED SUPPORT AND FOLLOW-UP

📋

Regular Check-ins

Monitor changes in mood, sleep, stress and daily functioning through regular confidential wellbeing check-ins.

🧑‍⚕️

Professional Support

Provide access to qualified medical and mental-health professionals when concerns arise.

🌍

Post-Mission Monitoring

Continue appropriate follow-up after returning to Earth to identify possible long-term changes in cognitive and behavioural health.

MISSION SAFETY NOTICE

ASTROGUARD provides educational wellbeing guidance. It does not diagnose psychiatric disorders or replace professional psychological or medical care. Mental-health concerns should be assessed by qualified professionals using established mission procedures.
